THE EVOLUTION OF BOARDSPORTS

From California beaches to Olympic slopes, discover how boardsports evolved into a worldwide culture.

1950s

Surfing Goes Mainstream

Modern surfing exploded in popularity after World War II, spreading from Hawaii and California to beaches around the world.

1970s

The Rise of Skateboarding

Skateboarding grew rapidly as surfers looked for a way to ride when the waves were flat, eventually becoming a culture of its own.

1970s

The Rise of Skateboarding

Skateboarding's most notable early and arguably first actual competition was held in Anaheim, California and broadcasted on live Television, contributing to the growth and popularity of the sport. The styles of skateboarding back then we heavily surf-inspired, and different from what we have now, but that doesn't discredit the impact this event had.

1970s

The First Snowboard

Snowboarding was invented in 1965, and though initially crude, it served as a way for surfers and skateboarders to hit the snowy mountains in a more comfortable form factor over two skis.

1980s

The First Snowboarding Competition

Just a decade and a half later, the first snowboarding competition would be organized in Colorado, called the "King of The Mountain" event. Despite it's low attendance of just 12 people, this was the first of many competitions that would spiral onto the massive recognition of snowboarding today.
